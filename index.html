Beginner's Academy

Essential knowledge and practices for intuitive reading and smart card interpretation.

✦ 1. Asking Empowering Questions

Tarot reads the energetic path you are currently on. Beginners get the smartest answers by framing inquiries constructively:

  • Avoid Yes/No: Instead of "Will I get the promotion?", ask "What energies should I embody to align with professional advancement?"
  • Focus on Self: Instead of "Does he love me?", ask "How can I foster mutual respect and deep connection in my relationship?"
  • Seek Actions: Instead of "When will my life change?", ask "What blocks must I clear to step into my highest path?"

✦ 2. The Language of Suits

The 75 cards are divided into Major Arcana (major life cycles) and 5 suits of Minor Arcana (everyday events):

  • Major Arcana (Spirit): Big milestones, karmic patterns, and spiritual changes.
  • Stars (Fire): Inspiration, ambition, action, creativity, and career drive.
  • Moons (Water): Feelings, intuition, secrets, relationships, and subconscious processing.
  • Comets (Air): Intellect, decisions, mental struggles, communication, and truth.
  • Worlds (Earth): Material comfort, physical body, money, business, and stability.
  • Angels (Light): Higher spiritual alignment, cosmic advice, and divine protection.

✦ 3. Deciphering Reversed Cards

When a card appears upside down, don't panic. It is not an evil omen! It signals:

  • Internal Focus: The energy of the card is directed inward. E.g., reversed Strength is about building inner confidence quietly.
  • Blocked Energy: You are experiencing blocks or resisting the natural flow of the card.
  • Excess or Deficit: The qualities of the card are either overemphasized or lacking.
  • Delays: Things are moving, but require patient preparation first.

✦ 4. Developing Your Intuition

To think smarter and read like a professional, integrate these intuitive habits:

  • Visual First: Look at the card symbol. What feeling, memory, or word pops into your head before reading the meaning?
  • Storytelling: In spread layouts, read cards together as a story. Connect the card in 'Past' to the card in 'Present'.
  • Reflective Journaling: Reviewing your journal logs weeks later builds a personal lexicon with the cards.
